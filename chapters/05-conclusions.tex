\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis presented a hybrid multimodal dual-branch retrieval-augmented pipeline for automated fact-checking of image-text claims. The system combines sparse lexical retrieval (SPLADE), dense semantic retrieval (BGE), and long-context visual retrieval (LongCLIP) with post-retrieval refinement stages (cross-encoder reranking and MMR diversification) to retrieve evidence from a multimodal knowledge store. A Vision-Language Model (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) then generates verification questions, extracts answers from retrieved evidence, and produces grounded verdicts with justifications. The pipeline was evaluated on the AVerImaTeC shared task benchmark, a multimodal fact verification challenge containing real-world claims from professional fact-checking websites.

\section{Answers to Research Questions}

\subsection*{RQ1: How do the individual retrieval modalities and post-retrieval stages contribute to downstream fact-checking performance?}

The experiments demonstrated that sparse, dense, and multimodal retrieval provide complementary strengths. LongCLIP significantly improved image-related evidence retrieval, while MMR diversification improved downstream reasoning by increasing evidence coverage. Together, these components produced the best overall fact-checking performance.

\subsection*{RQ2: How does iterative question-answer generation compare to single-pass QA generation?}

The results indicate that single-pass QA generation is more effective than iterative generation for multimodal fact-checking. Although iterative approaches provide greater flexibility, they are vulnerable to error propagation, whereas single-pass generation produces more consistent and robust verification outcomes.

\subsection*{RQ3: What is the effect of in-context learning on question generation quality and overall pipeline performance?}

In-context learning improved the overall robustness of the fact-checking pipeline by encouraging more structured and evidence-grounded reasoning. Although its effect on validation performance was mixed, the results showed improved generalization on unseen data, suggesting that in-context learning helps reduce reliance on dataset-specific biases.

\subsection*{RQ4: How does the proposed pipeline compare to state-of-the-art systems?}

The proposed pipeline achieved competitive test-set results compared to leading AVerImaTeC systems. These findings demonstrate that retrieval quality plays a critical role in multimodal fact-checking and that strong performance can be achieved using efficient open-source models when supported by effective evidence retrieval and ranking strategies.

\section{Key Findings}

Several cross-cutting findings emerge from this work:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Retrieval quality is the primary factor influencing fact-checking performance.} Improvements in evidence retrieval consistently translated into better downstream reasoning, verdict prediction, and justification generation, highlighting retrieval as a critical bottleneck in multimodal fact-checking.
    
    \item \textbf{Evidence diversity is as important as evidence relevance.} Diversification through MMR improved overall system performance by reducing redundancy and providing a broader evidence base for reasoning.
    
    \item \textbf{Multimodal retrieval is essential for image-based misinformation.} LongCLIP significantly improved the retrieval of image-related evidence, demonstrating the importance of long-context image-text alignment for multimodal fact verification.
    
    \item \textbf{Evaluation on small and imbalanced datasets should be interpreted cautiously.} The discrepancy between validation and test-set performance suggests that small evaluation sets may provide misleading estimates of generalization and can amplify dataset-specific biases.
    
    \item \textbf{Strong retrieval pipelines can compensate for smaller reasoning models.} The results demonstrate that competitive multimodal fact-checking performance can be achieved using open-source VLMs when combined with effective retrieval, reranking, and evidence selection strategies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Directions}
\label{sec:conclusions:future}

Several directions could address the limitations identified in this work:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Larger reasoning models.} Future work could investigate larger open-source VLMs to improve question generation, evidence interpretation, and complex multi-hop reasoning particularly for claims requiring complex temporal or causal inference, the category where our VLM reasoning errors occurred.
    
    \item \textbf{Adaptive retrieval routing.} Rather than applying all three retrievers uniformly, a learned routing mechanism could predict which retrieval modalities are most relevant for a given claim type, reducing computational cost while maintaining coverage.
    
    \item \textbf{Dynamic knowledge acquisition.} Extending the pipeline with real-time web retrieval could address evidence coverage limitations and improve performance on emerging claims and previously unseen topics.
    
    \item \textbf{Verdict calibration.} Future research could explore calibration techniques and balanced prompting strategies to improve generalization and reduce sensitivity to dataset biases.
    
    \item \textbf{Multilingual retrieval.} Integrating multilingual retrieval and reasoning models would enable verification of claims originating from diverse linguistic and cultural contexts.
    
    \item \textbf{Iterative retrieval with early stopping.} A hybrid approach that performs iterative retrieval but stops early when sufficient evidence is found could combine the theoretical advantages of iterative refinement with the practical benefits of single-pass efficiency, potentially avoiding the error propagation problem.
\end{itemize}
